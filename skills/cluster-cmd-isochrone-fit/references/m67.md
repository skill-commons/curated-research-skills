# M67: a Gaia DR3 observational example

Use this example to test fitting against real cluster photometry. Its member
selection is independent of the trial isochrone ages. M67 is demanding because
binaries, blue stragglers and uncertain turnoff physics affect the result.

## Retrieve and retain the observations

After following the skill's environment setup, stay in that workspace. Set
`SKILL_DIR` to the actual installed skill directory and retain `WORK_DIR` for
the output paths:

```bash
SKILL_DIR=/absolute/path/to/cluster-cmd-isochrone-fit
WORK_DIR="$PWD"
.venv/bin/python "$SKILL_DIR/scripts/fetch_m67.py" --out "$WORK_DIR/m67-members"
```

The helper uses the public CDS TAP endpoint and the Hunt & Reffert (2024)
`J/A+A/686/A42/members` table, with exact cluster name `NGC_2682`. It needs no
credentials. The bounded query returns at most 5,000 rows and the helper rejects
that limit as possible truncation; its byte limit is 3 MB. The verified response
on 2026-09-09 contained 1,844 unique Gaia DR3 members. It does not request
catalogue-derived cluster ages or stellar masses.

The new output directory contains:

- `source.csv`: exact downloaded TAP response, including every returned member.
- `query.adql`: the exact query.
- `stars.csv`: all members, propagated magnitude errors, diagnostic columns,
  selection flag and explicit exclusion reasons.
- `stars.json`: Gaia DR3 photometric-system declaration, units, query/source
  hashes, selection thresholds, counts and provenance.

Read `stars.csv` together with `stars.json`. The fitting engine applies its
separate, explicit CMD window; the retrieval helper applies no fitting window.
To convert the saved raw table with no network access into a new bundle:

```bash
.venv/bin/python "$SKILL_DIR/scripts/fetch_m67.py" \
  --from-raw "$WORK_DIR/m67-members/source.csv" \
  --out "$WORK_DIR/m67-members-replay"
```

The numerical `stars.csv` is reproducible. The new sidecar records its conversion
time and leaves original retrieval time unknown; retain the original sidecar for
the actual download time. Existing output directories are refused.

[Catalogue and data DOI](https://doi.org/10.26093/cds/vizier.36860042),
[CDS column descriptions](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/ftp/J/A+A/686/A42/ReadMe),
[Hunt & Reffert (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05143).

## Photometric and membership conventions

The observations are Gaia **DR3** G, BP and RP mean magnitudes on the Vega system.
DR3 and EDR3 share these passbands; DR2 uses different passbands. Match the model
system explicitly. The G-band issue present in EDR3 was corrected in DR3; do not
apply an EDR3 correction a second time. The adapter adds no empirical colour or
magnitude adjustment. [ESA DR3 description](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dr3),
[ESA passbands](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/edr3-passbands).

The adapter's declared quality selection is:

- HDBSCAN `Prob >= 0.5`;
- finite positive mean fluxes and errors, with flux/error **greater than 50**
  separately in G, BP and RP;
- `0 < RUWE < 1.4` and Gaia `NSS == 0`;
- finite plausible Gaia photometry and `G > 4` for the flux-excess prescription;
- corrected flux excess `abs(C*) < 3 sigma_C*(G)`.

The authors' `Prob` measures proximity to the cluster, without propagating Gaia
measurement errors. Their reliable-parameter sample uses the 0.5 threshold.
It is a useful selection score, not a calibrated membership probability to
multiply into a likelihood. [Hunt & Reffert (2023), Section 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.13424v2).

The adapter also excludes implausible magnitudes outside its broad validation
range (`4 < G < 25`, `-10 < BP, RP < 35`). These are input-validity checks, not
an age-sensitive selection. Missing or nonfinite photometry/errors remain blank
in excluded rows; all source bytes are retained. On the verified data, 807 members
pass `Prob >= 0.5`, and 685 pass the complete adapter quality selection. These
are observations from that retrieval, not fixed counts a future archive must
return.

The flux errors are errors on mean flux, in electrons per second. The propagated
errors in `stars.csv` are `sigma_m = (2.5 / ln(10)) sigma_F / F`. For BP-RP use
`sqrt(sigma_BP**2 + sigma_RP**2)`, assuming independent band errors. Common
calibration uncertainty and model mismatch are not included. G versus BP-RP
does not have the exact shared-band covariance of G versus BP-G.

For colour `x = BP-RP`, the quality diagnostic is
`C* = (FBP + FRP) / FG - f(x)`:

| Colour range | f(x) |
| --- | --- |
| x < 0.5 | 1.154360 + 0.033772 x + 0.032277 x² |
| 0.5 <= x < 4 | 1.162004 + 0.011464 x + 0.049255 x² - 0.005879 x³ |
| x >= 4 | 1.057572 + 0.140537 x |

`sigma_C*(G) = 0.0059898 + 8.817481e-12 G**7.618399` is the population scatter
envelope, not an individual error bar. The prescription applies only to `G > 4`.
The three-sigma choice is our conservative selection. [Riello et al. (2021),
Table 2 and Equation 18](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2021/05/aa39587-20.pdf).

`NSS == 0` means no information in the relevant non-single-star tables, not
verified singleness. RUWE and excess cuts likewise do not identify every binary.
Keep excluded stars visible in the full CMD where their photometry is finite.
Compare a stricter membership selection and different fitting-window edges to
measure sensitivity; never change cuts to match an expected age.

## Distance, extinction and composition

An independent Gaia DR3 parallax constraint from Reyes et al. (2024) is a
zero-point-corrected mean parallax of `1.195 +/- 0.027 mas`, corresponding to true
distance modulus `mu0 = 9.614 +/- 0.049 mag`. Their final CMD uses **DR2
photometry**, despite DR3-based membership; use the distance reference, not their
magnitudes as DR3 data. They discuss near-solar initial composition and
evolution-dependent surface abundances. Their M67-tailored age is a comparison,
not a fitting target. [Reyes et al. (2024), Sections 2.2 and 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.03526v1).

A transparent first run can fix distance modulus near 9.614 and test a broader
range around it, for example 9.50--9.75, while holding metallicity and extinction
explicit. This interval is an illustrative sensitivity range, not a confidence
interval. `plx` and `plx_error` in the adapter output remain the individual Gaia
values as published. Do not invert uncorrected member parallaxes to define the
cluster distance, or shrink all parallax uncertainty as `1/sqrt(N)` while ignoring
the shared zero point. The official source-dependent correction uses magnitude,
colour, position and astrometric solution type. [ESA parallax guidance and
correction tool](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dr3-software-tools).

For a low-reddening baseline, `E(B-V)=0.041` and `R_V=3.1` imply `A_V=0.1271 mag`.
The E(B-V) value is the Taylor (2007) determination discussed by Reyes et al.;
test extinction sensitivity rather than treating it as exact. Prefer a model
provider's spectrum-dependent extinction treatment in the correct Gaia filters.
`A_V`, `A_G`, `E(B-V)` and `E(BP-RP)` are different quantities. Apply extinction
once, to either the model or the observations according to the fitting contract.
A uniform screen ignores differential reddening.

Near-solar initial metallicity is a sensible starting hypothesis. Specify the
provider's actual `Z`, helium law and solar mixture, or the exact native `[M/H]`
or `[Fe/H]` input. Do not silently equate surface [Fe/H], initial total heavy
element fraction and provider-specific solar scaling. Repeat with plausible
nearby metallicities; the resulting age change is part of model sensitivity.

## What this example can establish

For the initial M67 experiment, the following window was declared after
inspecting the observed CMD and before computing trial-age scores: `11.5 < G <
15` and `0.4 < BP-RP < 1.5`. It includes the observed turnoff, subgiants and lower
red giant branch; it excludes the distinct brighter red-clump group and the
very blue candidates. It is specific to M67, not a generic cluster cut.

With a model bundle covering `logAge=9.0--10.0`, native `[M/H]=0`, and
`A_V=0.1271`, a worked baseline is:

```bash
.venv/bin/python "$SKILL_DIR/scripts/fit_cmd.py" \
  --stars "$WORK_DIR/m67-members/stars.csv" \
  --models "$WORK_DIR/parsec-grid/model.csv" \
  --output "$WORK_DIR/m67-baseline" \
  --distance-modulus 9.614 --g-range 11.5,15 --color-range 0.4,1.5 \
  --mh 0 --av 0.1271 --phases 1,2,3 \
  --floor-g 0.03 --floor-color 0.02 --bootstrap 100 --seed 2026
```

The initial sensitivity plan changes the faint G limit to 14.5 and 15.5 and
also compares a broader `10 < G < 15` window without a colour restriction.
Independently vary modulus to 9.565/9.663, extinction to 0.10/0.15, and native
metallicity to -0.1/+0.1, using actual corresponding model grids. Also compare
`Prob >= 0.8` and doubled metric floors. These are declared sensitivity
experiments, not formal prior draws or attempts to tune the literature age.

The turnoff and subgiants constrain age; the crowded lower main sequence mostly
constrains distance, colour offsets and the adopted model. Plot the full member
CMD and the independently declared fitting-window boundary. Keep that observed
window unchanged across all trial ages. Inspect residuals and minima at the age
or distance boundary before quoting a result.

An equal-mass unresolved binary lies `2.5 log10(2) = 0.752575 mag` above its
single-star components at unchanged colour. That line is a useful diagnostic,
not a full binary model: unequal-mass binaries move in colour too. Blue stragglers
can resemble a much younger turnoff. A robust geometric score reduces outlier
influence but does not establish binary identities or a normalized population
likelihood. Bootstrap spread is conditional sample sensitivity, not the total
uncertainty in stellar age. For a full population likelihood, consult
[Naylor & Jeffries (2006)](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0609764).
